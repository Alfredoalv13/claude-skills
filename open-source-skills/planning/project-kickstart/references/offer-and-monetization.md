# Offer, Market & Money Model (Alex Hormozi frameworks)

Source frameworks: $100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models. Applied at
Phase 1, before architecture. The output file is `launch/offer.md` plus
foundation doc 40 (Offer & Monetization Strategy).

Why first: fixing the offer is cheaper than fixing the product. If the offer
scores badly, change the target market or the promise — not the tech stack.

---

## 1. Market selection (before the offer)

Score the intended market 1–5 on each. A great offer to a bad market still fails.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain | Do they desperately need (not just want) this solved? |
| Purchasing power | Can they afford to pay what makes this worth building? |
| Easy to target | Do they gather somewhere reachable (channels, communities, lists)? |
| Growing | Is the market expanding, flat, or shrinking? |

Order of leverage (strongest first): market > offer > persuasion. Niche down
until the user can name the specific avatar ("newly-listed Miami realtors", not
"real estate professionals"). Riches are in the niches: the same product
commands 10–100x the price when positioned for a specific avatar's specific
problem.

## 2. The Value Equation

```
          Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                  Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

Interrogate the product concept against all four levers:
- **Dream outcome** — state it in the avatar's words, as status/identity change,
  not features. ("Never lose a lead again", not "CRM with reminders".)
- **Perceived likelihood** — proof, guarantees, testimonials, "done-for-you"
  elements, onboarding that produces a fast first win.
- **Time delay** — what is the time-to-first-value? Every day between signup and
  first win kills conversions. This SHAPES THE MVP: the fastest path to the
  dream outcome is the MVP; everything else is later epics.
- **Effort & sacrifice** — what must the user give up or do? Imports, setup,
  behavior change. Each removal is a feature candidate with high offer-value.

The denominator matters more than the numerator for software: speed-to-value
and low effort beat bigger promises.

## 3. Building the offer stack (Grand Slam Offer)

1. List every problem/obstacle the avatar hits before, during, and after using
   the product (dozens — be exhaustive).
2. Turn each problem into a solution statement.
3. Choose delivery vehicles for each solution (product feature, template pack,
   onboarding call, community, done-for-you migration, course, checklist...).
   Prefer high-value + low-marginal-cost vehicles (software, recordings,
   templates, community) — that's what makes SaaS margins.
4. Trim to the stack that maximizes value per unit of delivery cost.

**Enhancers** — apply each deliberately, ethically:
- **Scarcity** (limited seats/capacity — only if true)
- **Urgency** (cohort start dates, founding-member pricing windows, seasonal)
- **Bonuses** (name them, price them individually; the stack should feel worth
  10x the price; bonuses > discounts, always)
- **Guarantees** — pick one: unconditional (strongest), conditional ("do X, get
  Y or refund + Z"), anti-guarantee ("all sales final because..."), or implied
  performance (pay-per-result). Risk reversal is usually the single biggest
  conversion lever for a new product with no reputation.
- **Naming (M-A-G-I-C)**: Magnetic reason-why + Avatar + Goal + Interval +
  Container. ("14-Day Miami Realtor Listing Engine", not "Pro Plan".)

## 4. Pricing

- Price on value, not cost. Charge what the dream outcome is worth, then make
  the value 10x the price with the stack — don't lower the price to close the
  gap ("the virtuous cycle of price": higher price → more investment by the
  client → better results → better proof → higher price).
- Never compete on price; compete on category-of-one positioning. If prospects
  can compare you 1:1 with an alternative, the offer isn't differentiated yet.
- For SaaS: anchor tiers to avatar outcomes, not feature counts.

## 5. Money model ($100M Money Models — offer sequencing)

Design the SEQUENCE of offers, not just one. Goal: **client-financed
acquisition** — gross profit from a new customer in the first 30 days ≥ the
cost to acquire them (ideally ≥ 2x CAC), so growth funds itself.

| Slot | Role | SaaS examples |
|---|---|---|
| Attraction offer | Get them in the door; monetize the "yes" | Free trial + paid fast-start setup, $7 template pack, paid workshop, founding-member deal |
| Upsell | Immediately after the first yes, offer the bigger/faster version | Annual prepay, done-for-you onboarding, higher tier |
| Downsell | Rescue the "no" with a smaller commitment | Monthly instead of annual, lite tier, payment plan, trial extension |
| Continuity | Recurring revenue backbone | The subscription itself, community, ongoing service add-on |

Record in `launch/offer.md`: the attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity
map, target price points, projected 30-day gross profit per customer, and
estimated CAC per channel.

## 6. Offer scorecard (0–10)

Score one point per "yes"; iterate rows to "yes" until ≥ 8:

1. Named avatar in a market with pain + purchasing power + reachability + growth
2. Dream outcome stated in avatar's words
3. Proof/likelihood elements exist (or a plan to manufacture them pre-launch)
4. Time-to-first-value under 10 minutes (or best-in-category)
5. Effort/sacrifice reduced vs. every alternative (incl. spreadsheets/doing nothing)
6. Offer stack ≥ 3 delivery vehicles beyond the core product
7. A real risk-reversal guarantee
8. Ethical scarcity and/or urgency mechanism
9. Value-based price with 10x perceived value gap
10. Category-of-one: cannot be price-compared 1:1 to an alternative

Bands: 9–10 irresistible · 7–8 launchable, keep iterating · 5–6 sound but
missing risk-reversal/bonuses/urgency · ≤3 commodity — go back to market
selection.

## 7. Leads preview ($100M Leads — feeds Phase 6)

Decided now because it shapes the product surface (public pages, SEO, sharing):
- **Lead magnet**: solve one narrow problem of the avatar completely, for free,
  that reveals the bigger problem the paid product solves. Ship it as part of
  the product's public surface (free tool, calculator, template, audit).
- **Core Four channels**: warm outreach, content, cold outreach, paid ads.
  Pick ONE primary channel for launch based on where the avatar already is;
  note it in `launch/offer.md` so SEO/LLMSEO strategy (doc 08) and public-page
  architecture (doc 04) support it from day one.
- Give-until-they-ask: the free surface should be genuinely valuable alone.
