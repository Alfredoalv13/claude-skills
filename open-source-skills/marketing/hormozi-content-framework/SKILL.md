# Hormozi Content Framework

## Purpose
Setup: replace `{{brand}}`, `{{audience}}` and `{{product}}` below with your own. Every piece of content for {{brand}} should follow these principles. This isn't a style guide — it's the operating system for how we think about content as a value-delivery vehicle.

## Core Philosophy

**Content IS the product.** Not a funnel to the product. Not a teaser. The content itself should be so valuable that people would pay for it. When your free content is better than other people's paid content, trust compounds and sales follow.

## The $100M Offers Framework Applied to Content

### 1. The Value Equation (Apply to Every Post)

Every piece of content is an "offer" to the reader's attention. Score it against Hormozi's Value Equation:

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

**To maximize content value:**
- **Dream Outcome** → Make the benefit specific and desirable. Not "save time" but "get your Monday inbox to zero in 20 minutes."
- **Perceived Likelihood** → Give proof, steps, or frameworks that make the reader think "I could actually do this." Specificity builds belief.
- **Time Delay** → Shrink the time to result. "Try this today" beats "implement over 6 months." Immediate wins > long-term promises.
- **Effort & Sacrifice** → Lower the barrier. "Paste this prompt" beats "build a custom automation." Copy-paste > learn-then-build.

**Content quality test:** If a reader can't get a result within the same day they read it, the content isn't actionable enough.

### 2. The Grand Slam Offer Structure (For Lead Magnets & Guides)

When creating lead magnets, free guides, or any gated content:

1. **Name it well** — The name should communicate the dream outcome, the time frame, and what they avoid. Example: "The 5-Minute AI Quick Win Checklist" (dream: quick wins, time: 5 min, avoids: complexity)
2. **Stack the value** — Bundle multiple deliverables. A checklist alone is OK. A checklist + prompt library + before/after examples + SOP template = irresistible.
3. **Remove risk** — "No email list, no follow-up campaign" or "No tools to buy" removes the risk of being sold to.
4. **Add scarcity/urgency only when real** — Never manufacture fake urgency. {{brand}}'s trust positioning means we only use urgency when it's genuine (limited spots, a real deadline, etc.)

### 3. The Starving Crowd Principle (For Audience Targeting)

Don't create content for everyone. Create content for people who are **already in pain**:
- Business owners who just lost a client because their follow-up was too slow
- Accountants drowning in bank statement conversions during tax season
- Contractors who leave money on the table because they forget add-ons

**Content targeting test:** Can you name the specific moment in the reader's day when they'd feel this pain? If not, the content isn't targeted enough.

## The $100M Leads Framework Applied to Content

### 1. The Four Core Ways to Get Leads (Content Strategy)

Every content channel maps to one of Hormozi's four methods:

| Method | How We Use It |
|--------|--------------|
| **Warm Outreach** | Email sequences to existing contacts (your outreach pipeline) |
| **Cold Outreach** | DMs, cold email, Reddit comments that provide value first |
| **Content (Free)** | X, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube — the organic engine |
| **Paid Ads** | Future state, only after organic proves what converts |

**Content's job in the leads framework:** Create so much free value that warm and cold outreach feel like a continuation of the help, not a pitch.

### 2. The Give → Give → Give → Ask Model

Content ratio: **4:1 value to ask.** For every 4 posts that teach, share, or help without asking for anything, 1 post can promote a product.

But even the "ask" posts should lead with value:
- ❌ "Buy our email playbook — $29"
- ✅ "Your follow-up emails are costing you deals. Here's the exact prompt to fix it. If you want 9 more like it with real scenarios, we built a playbook for that. {{link}}"

### 3. The Lead Magnet Quality Standard

Principle (from $100M Leads, paraphrased): a lead magnet should be good enough that people would pay for it.

Your free guides and checklists must pass this test. Every free resource should make the reader think: "If this is what they give away for free, what's in the paid version?"

### 4. Content as Proof

Every post is proof that you know what you're talking about. Hormozi's principle: **you earn the right to sell by proving you can help.**

Types of proof in content:
- **Teaching proof** — Show you understand their problem by describing it better than they can
- **Framework proof** — Give them a mental model they can use immediately
- **Specificity proof** — Generic advice = no proof. Specific, testable advice = proof of expertise
- **Vulnerability proof** — Admit what you don't know, what failed, what's hard. This builds more trust than perfection.

## Content Creation Rules (Apply These to EVERY Piece)

### Rule 1: The Actionability Test
> "If someone reads this and can't do something differently in the next 60 minutes, it failed."

Every post needs a **do-this-now** element: a prompt to paste, a question to ask, a habit to start, a tool to try, a framework to apply.

### Rule 2: The Specificity Standard
> "Generic advice is invisible. Specific advice is magnetic."

- ❌ "Use AI to save time on emails"
- ✅ "Paste your last 3 client follow-ups into AI with this prompt: 'Analyze my communication style. Describe my tone, sentence length, and how I open/close.' Save the output. Reference it every time you ask AI to draft an email."

### Rule 3: The Stranger Test
> "Would someone who has never heard of you get value from this post?"

Content should work for cold audiences. Product mentions should be absent from 80%+ of posts. The brand earns recognition through the quality of free value, not through self-referencing.

### Rule 4: The Trust-First Filter
> "Say what worked AND what didn't. Recommend what's free before what's paid. Admit when you don't know."

Suggested positioning (adapt to your brand): tool-agnostic, affiliate-free, implementation over theory. Every piece of content should reinforce that we're the honest guide in a market full of hype.

### Rule 5: The Repetition-Without-Duplication Rule
> "Teach the same concept 100 different ways. Never use the same words twice."

People need to hear a message 7-15 times before it clicks. Each time, use:
- A different scenario (email for a plumber vs. email for a CPA)
- A different format (tip vs. story vs. framework vs. question vs. before/after)
- A different entry point (the pain vs. the solution vs. the result vs. the mistake)

Same lesson. Fresh angle. Every time.

### Rule 6: The Platform Fit Rule

| Platform | Max Length | Style | Format |
|----------|-----------|-------|--------|
| **X (unverified)** | 280 chars | Punchy, one insight, immediate value | Single tweet, no threads unless verified |
| **LinkedIn** | 3,000 chars | Conversational, story-driven, deeper frameworks | Text posts, carousels for step-by-step |
| **Instagram** | 2,200 chars caption | Visual-first, caption supports the visual | Carousels (educational), Reels (demos), Static (quotes/frameworks) |
| **YouTube** | No limit | Long-form, walkthrough, screen share | Tutorials, myth-busters, deep dives |
| **Email** | ~300 words | Personal, 1-to-1, plain text feel | Value delivery, relationship building |

## Content Pillar Rotation

Rotate content across these pillars to avoid monotony:

1. **Implementation** (40%) — Step-by-step, prompts, workflows, tutorials
2. **Trust & Transparency** (20%) — When NOT to use AI, honest takes, limitations, failures
3. **Decision Framework** (20%) — How to evaluate, compare, choose, prioritize
4. **Foundations** (15%) — Mindset shifts, core concepts, "why this matters"
5. **Promo** (5%) — Free guide CTAs, product mentions, always value-led

---

## Quality Checklist (Run Before Publishing)

Before any content goes live, it must pass ALL of these:

- [ ] **Actionable?** Reader can do something different today.
- [ ] **Specific?** Includes a scenario, example, prompt, or number — not generic advice.
- [ ] **Truthful?** No fabricated stories, fake metrics, or aspirational claims presented as fact.
- [ ] **Platform-fit?** Correct length, format, and style for the channel.
- [ ] **Stranger-friendly?** Works without knowing who {{brand}} is.
- [ ] **Value equation positive?** High dream outcome + likelihood, low time delay + effort.
- [ ] **Not a duplicate?** Same concept is fine. Same words are not.
- [ ] **Trust-building?** Reinforces honest, tool-agnostic, implementation-first positioning.

## Non-English / Bilingual Content Note (example: Spanish)

Spanish content follows all the same rules. Additionally:
- Write from scratch in Spanish, don't translate English posts
- Use tú, not usted (conversational, peer-to-peer)
- Reference LATAM business contexts where relevant (not just US examples)
- Check competition in your language market; underserved languages are an opportunity
